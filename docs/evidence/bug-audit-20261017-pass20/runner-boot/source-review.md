## Rejected or bounded hypotheses

- A failed full-log assessment could silently reuse a successful tail: the
  actual Wizard supplies an explicit empty assessment when the runner's ready
  assessment is unavailable; it does not pass None and trigger fallback.
- A new launch could inherit an earlier verdict/readiness or log: start resets
  old state and readiness, retains a fresh log inode, and binds the active
  request; pre-existing duplicate launch refuses before changing its log.
- A late stop could rewrite an already observed successful exit as cancelled:
  pre-signal status and the post-wait success race are explicitly classified
  through terminal method evidence. Uncertain termination retains ownership.
- COMPLETE or an evidence sidecar alone could prove erase/safe removal: both
  authenticate content only; classification checks log facts and removal is a
  later live receipt after final unmount. Recovery rechecks its journal digest.
- A missing/truncated log could prove that no data changed: those exceptional
  nothing-erased explanations require the exact authenticated original marker;
  contradictory activity stays indeterminate, and recovery/export revalidate.
- Bare model continuations could provide native no-erasure/readiness origin:
  current origin guards and retained native context are present. This review
  did not reopen the prior pinned-source model-field investigation or claim a
  universal NVMe model length. No new upstream fact was assumed or browsed.
- Fixed-width shared status cells could credit another device: arbitrary
  punctuation-bearing logged names are recognized, projection preserves a
  colliding path, and unassignable positive cells are unavailable.
- Optional power telemetry could decide erase safety: the PowerStatus contract
  explicitly stays advisory; unknown/unreadable readings replace old reports.
